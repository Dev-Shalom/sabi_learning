## Question

6.25 g of impure calcium trioxocarbonate(IV) is heated strongly until no more gas is given off. 1.12 dm3 of carbon(IV) oxide, measured at s.t.p., is collected. The equation is

CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2

Assuming the impurities do not decompose, calculate the percentage purity of the sample. [C = 12, O = 16, Ca = 40; molar volume of a gas at s.t.p. = 22.4 dm3/mol]

## Solution

1. The gas came only from the CaCO3, so work back from it to the mass of pure CaCO3 in the sample.
2. Moles of CO2 = 1.12 / 22.4 = 0.05 mol.
3. From the equation, 1 mole of CaCO3 gives 1 mole of CO2, so the moles of CaCO3 that decomposed are the same.
4. Moles of CaCO3 = 0.05 mol.
5. Molar mass of CaCO3 = 40 + 12 + (3 x 16) = 100 g/mol.
6. Mass of pure CaCO3 = 0.05 x 100 = 5 g.
7. Percentage purity = (mass of pure substance / mass of sample) x 100.
8. Purity = (5 / 6.25) x 100 = 80 per cent.
9. So 1.25 g of the sample was impurity.

**Answer: 80 per cent pure**
