## Question

One cubic decimetre of a sample of hard water contains 0.162 g of calcium hydrogentrioxocarbonate(IV), Ca(HCO3)2, and 0.12 g of magnesium tetraoxosulphate(VI), MgSO4. Calculate (a) the total hardness in mol/dm3 of calcium and magnesium ions, (b) the total hardness in parts per million expressed as CaCO3, (c) the hardness remaining after the water has been boiled, in ppm. [H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Mg = 24, S = 32, Ca = 40. Take 1 dm3 of water to have a mass of 1 kg, so that 1 ppm is 1 mg/dm3.]

## Solution

1. Part (a). Hardness is caused by the calcium and magnesium ions, and each of these salts gives one such ion per formula unit, so find the moles of each salt.
2. Molar mass of Ca(HCO3)2 = 40 + 2 x (1 + 12 + (3 x 16)) = 40 + (2 x 61) = 162 g/mol.
3. Moles of Ca(HCO3)2 = 0.162 / 162 = 0.001 mol, which is 0.001 mol of Ca2+.
4. Molar mass of MgSO4 = 24 + 32 + (4 x 16) = 120 g/mol.
5. Moles of MgSO4 = 0.12 / 120 = 0.001 mol, which is 0.001 mol of Mg2+.
6. Total hardness = 0.001 + 0.001 = 0.002 mol/dm3.
7. Part (b). Hardness is conventionally quoted as though all of it were calcium trioxocarbonate(IV), so convert the same number of moles into a mass of CaCO3.
8. Molar mass of CaCO3 = 40 + 12 + (3 x 16) = 100 g/mol.
9. Mass as CaCO3 = 0.002 x 100 = 0.2 g/dm3, which is 200 mg/dm3, that is 200 ppm.
10. Part (c). Boiling decomposes only the hydrogentrioxocarbonate(IV), Ca(HCO3)2 -> CaCO3 + H2O + CO2, so the temporary hardness is precipitated out and the MgSO4 is left in solution.
11. Remaining hardness = 0.001 x 100 = 0.1 g/dm3 = 100 ppm, and it is permanent hardness, needing washing soda or an ion-exchange column to remove it.

**Answer: (a) 0.002 mol/dm3  (b) 200 ppm as CaCO3  (c) 100 ppm of permanent hardness**
