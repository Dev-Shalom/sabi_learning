## Question

100 cm3 of 1 mol/dm3 hydrochloric acid is mixed with 100 cm3 of 1 mol/dm3 sodium hydroxide solution in a plastic cup. The temperature rises by 6.8 degrees Celsius. The equation is

HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O

Calculate (a) the heat given out, (b) the heat of neutralisation in kJ/mol. [Take the density of the mixture as 1 g/cm3 and the specific heat capacity of water as 4200 J/kg/K, that is 4.2 J/g/K]

## Solution

1. Part (a). The heat is taken up by the mixture, so use Q = mc x change in temperature, with m the mass of the mixture.
2. Mass = 100 + 100 = 200 cm3, and at 1 g/cm3 that is 200 g, or 0.2 kg.
3. Q = 0.2 x 4200 x 6.8.
4. Q = 5712 J.
5. Part (b). Find how many moles of water were formed, then divide.
6. Moles of HCl = 1 x (100 / 1000) = 0.1 mol, and the same for NaOH. The ratio in the equation is 1 to 1, so 0.1 mol of water is formed and neither reactant is in excess.
7. Heat per mole = 5712 / 0.1 = 57120 J/mol, which is 57.12 kJ/mol.
8. The temperature rose, so the reaction is exothermic and the heat of neutralisation is written with a negative sign: -57.12 kJ/mol.
9. This is close to the accepted value for a strong acid with a strong alkali, because in every such pair the reaction is really the same one, H+ + OH- -> H2O.

**Answer: (a) 5712 J  (b) -57.12 kJ/mol**
