## Question

(a) A solution of tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid, H2SO4, has a concentration of 0.5 mol/dm3. Express this in g/dm3. (b) A solution of sodium hydroxide, NaOH, contains 8 g/dm3. Express this in mol/dm3. [H = 1, O = 16, Na = 23, S = 32]

## Solution

1. The two units are linked by the molar mass: concentration in g/dm3 = concentration in mol/dm3 x molar mass.
2. Part (a). Molar mass of H2SO4 = (2 x 1) + 32 + (4 x 16) = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 g/mol.
3. Concentration = 0.5 x 98 = 49 g/dm3.
4. Part (b). Going the other way, divide by the molar mass.
5. Molar mass of NaOH = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40 g/mol.
6. Concentration = 8 / 40 = 0.2 mol/dm3.

**Answer: (a) 49 g/dm3  (b) 0.2 mol/dm3**
