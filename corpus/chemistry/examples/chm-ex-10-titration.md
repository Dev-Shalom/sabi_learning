## Question

25 cm3 of 0.1 mol/dm3 sodium trioxocarbonate(IV) solution, Na2CO3, required 20 cm3 of hydrochloric acid for complete neutralisation, using methyl orange as indicator. The equation is

Na2CO3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + H2O + CO2

Calculate the concentration of the acid in (a) mol/dm3, (b) g/dm3. [H = 1, Cl = 35.5]

## Solution

1. Start from the solution whose concentration is known.
2. Moles of Na2CO3 = concentration x volume in dm3 = 0.1 x (25 / 1000) = 0.0025 mol.
3. The equation gives the mole ratio: 1 mole of Na2CO3 reacts with 2 moles of HCl. This is the step most often skipped.
4. Moles of HCl = 2 x 0.0025 = 0.005 mol.
5. That amount of acid was in 20 cm3, which is 20 / 1000 = 0.02 dm3.
6. Part (a). Concentration = 0.005 / 0.02 = 0.25 mol/dm3.
7. Part (b). Molar mass of HCl = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5 g/mol.
8. Concentration in g/dm3 = 0.25 x 36.5 = 9.125 g/dm3.
9. Methyl orange is the right indicator here because the salt formed, NaCl, leaves the solution at the end point acidic rather than neutral.

**Answer: (a) 0.25 mol/dm3  (b) 9.125 g/dm3**
