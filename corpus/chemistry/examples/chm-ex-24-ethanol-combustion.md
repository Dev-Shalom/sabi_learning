## Question

9.2 g of ethanol, C2H5OH, is burnt completely in oxygen. The equation is

C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O

Calculate (a) the volume of carbon(IV) oxide produced, measured at s.t.p., (b) its mass. [H = 1, C = 12, O = 16; molar volume of a gas at s.t.p. = 22.4 dm3/mol]

## Solution

1. The equation is a statement about moles, so turn the mass of ethanol into moles first.
2. Molar mass of C2H5OH = (2 x 12) + (6 x 1) + 16 = 24 + 6 + 16 = 46 g/mol.
3. Moles of ethanol = 9.2 / 46 = 0.2 mol.
4. From the equation, 1 mole of ethanol gives 2 moles of CO2, because both carbon atoms end up as carbon(IV) oxide.
5. Moles of CO2 = 2 x 0.2 = 0.4 mol.
6. Part (a). Volume at s.t.p. = moles x 22.4 = 0.4 x 22.4 = 8.96 dm3.
7. Part (b). Molar mass of CO2 = 12 + (2 x 16) = 44 g/mol.
8. Mass = 0.4 x 44 = 17.6 g.
9. Complete combustion is assumed because oxygen is in plenty. In a limited supply the ethanol would give carbon(II) oxide or soot instead, and this arithmetic would not apply.

**Answer: (a) 8.96 dm3 at s.t.p.  (b) 17.6 g of carbon(IV) oxide**
