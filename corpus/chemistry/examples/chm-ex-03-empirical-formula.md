## Question

An oxide of sulphur contains 40 per cent sulphur and 60 per cent oxygen by mass. Determine its empirical formula. [O = 16, S = 32]

## Solution

1. Take 100 g of the compound, so each percentage is a mass in grams.
2. Divide each mass by the relative atomic mass of that element to get the number of moles of atoms.
3. Sulphur: 40 / 32 = 1.25 mol of atoms.
4. Oxygen: 60 / 16 = 3.75 mol of atoms.
5. Divide both by the smaller of the two, 1.25, to get the simplest ratio.
6. Sulphur: 1.25 / 1.25 = 1. Oxygen: 3.75 / 1.25 = 3.
7. The ratio of sulphur to oxygen atoms is 1 : 3, so the empirical formula is SO3.
8. Check it. The formula mass is 32 + (3 x 16) = 80, and 32/80 x 100 = 40 per cent sulphur, which is what the question gave.

**Answer: SO3**
