## Question

6.5 g of zinc is added to 50 cm3 of 2 mol/dm3 hydrochloric acid. The equation is

Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2

Determine (a) which reactant is the limiting reactant, (b) the volume of hydrogen produced at s.t.p. [Zn = 65; molar volume of a gas at s.t.p. = 22.4 dm3/mol]

## Solution

1. Part (a). Convert both reactants to moles.
2. Moles of Zn = 6.5 / 65 = 0.1 mol.
3. Moles of HCl = concentration x volume in dm3 = 2 x (50 / 1000) = 0.1 mol.
4. Now divide each by its coefficient in the equation. The smaller result is the reactant that runs out first.
5. Zn: 0.1 / 1 = 0.1. HCl: 0.1 / 2 = 0.05.
6. 0.05 is the smaller, so the hydrochloric acid is the limiting reactant and some zinc will be left undissolved.
7. Part (b). The limiting reactant decides how much product forms. From the equation 2 moles of HCl give 1 mole of H2.
8. Moles of H2 = 0.1 / 2 = 0.05 mol.
9. Volume at s.t.p. = 0.05 x 22.4 = 1.12 dm3.

**Answer: (a) the hydrochloric acid  (b) 1.12 dm3 of hydrogen at s.t.p.**
