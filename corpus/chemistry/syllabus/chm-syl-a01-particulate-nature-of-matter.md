## What the syllabus requires

State that matter is made of small discrete particles and give the evidence for
it. Distinguish elements, compounds and mixtures, and physical from chemical
change. Describe the three states of matter in terms of particle arrangement and
motion, and the changes between them. Choose and describe a method of separating
a named mixture, and calculate the composition of a mixture from masses
recovered.

## Evidence that matter is particulate

Brownian motion is the ceaseless, random, zig-zag movement of smoke particles
seen through a microscope in a smoke cell. The smoke particles are visible; the
air molecules knocking them about are not. The motion is evidence that the air is
made of separate particles in constant random motion.

Diffusion is the spreading of one substance through another of its own accord.
Ammonia gas released at one end of a tube is detected at the other end without
anything stirring it, because its molecules move at random and spread out.

Dissolving is a third piece of evidence. A crystal of potassium manganate(VII)
dropped into water colours the whole beaker, so the solid must break up into
particles small enough to spread between the water particles.

## Elements, compounds and mixtures

An element contains only one kind of atom and cannot be split into simpler
substances by chemical means. A compound contains two or more elements chemically
combined in a fixed proportion by mass, and its properties are unlike those of
its elements. A mixture contains two or more substances that are not chemically
combined, in any proportion, and each keeps its own properties.

Iron and sulphur illustrate the difference. A mixture of iron filings and sulphur
powder can be separated with a magnet, and the iron still rusts. Heated together
they form iron(II) sulphide, FeS, a grey solid that is not magnetic and from
which the iron cannot be picked out with a magnet.

A physical change alters only the form of a substance and is usually easy to
reverse: melting ice, dissolving salt, magnetising iron. A chemical change
produces a new substance, is usually accompanied by an energy change, and is not
reversed by simply undoing the conditions: burning magnesium, rusting iron,
neutralising an acid.

## The three states, and the changes between them

In a solid the particles are closely packed in a fixed pattern and only vibrate
about fixed positions, so a solid has a fixed shape and volume. In a liquid the
particles are still close together but can slide past one another, so a liquid
has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container. In a gas the particles
are far apart and move rapidly in all directions, so a gas has neither fixed
shape nor fixed volume and can be compressed.

Melting and boiling absorb heat; freezing and condensation release it. During
melting or boiling the temperature stays constant, because the heat supplied goes
into separating the particles rather than into speeding them up. Sublimation is
the direct change from solid to vapour, shown by iodine, ammonium chloride and
solid carbon dioxide.

## Separating mixtures, with the arithmetic

The method follows from the difference in properties:

- Filtration separates an insoluble solid from a liquid.
- Evaporation or crystallisation recovers a dissolved solid from its solution.
- Simple distillation recovers the solvent from a solution.
- Fractional distillation separates miscible liquids of different boiling points,
  such as ethanol from water, or the fractions of crude oil.
- Sublimation separates a solid that sublimes, such as ammonium chloride, from
  one that does not.
- Chromatography separates dissolved substances that travel different distances
  up a paper.
- A separating funnel separates immiscible liquids, such as oil and water.

Worked figure. A 50 g sample of a sand and common salt mixture is stirred with
water, filtered, and the filtrate evaporated to dryness. The residue in the
filter paper dries to 20 g of sand, and the evaporating dish holds 30 g of salt.
The mixture was therefore 30/50 times 100 = 60 per cent salt and
20/50 times 100 = 40 per cent sand by mass. The two masses add to 50 g, which is
the check that nothing was lost.

## Not on this syllabus

The kinetic-theory derivation of the gas pressure equation, the Maxwell-Boltzmann
distribution as a mathematical function, intermolecular potential-energy curves,
X-ray crystallography and unit-cell calculations, and the quantitative treatment
of Brownian motion by the Einstein relation are not examined here.
