## What the syllabus requires

Describe a reversible reaction and explain what dynamic equilibrium means. Write
the equilibrium constant expression Kc for a given equation. Calculate Kc from
equilibrium concentrations. State Le Chatelier's principle and use it to predict
the effect of a change in concentration, pressure or temperature. Apply it to the
Haber and Contact processes.

## Reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium

A reversible reaction is one that can go in either direction, written with a
double arrow. Heating hydrated copper(II) tetraoxosulphate(VI) drives off water
to give the white anhydrous salt; adding water turns it blue again.

In a closed system a reversible reaction reaches dynamic equilibrium. It is an
equilibrium because the concentrations of reactants and products stop changing.
It is dynamic because the forward and backward reactions have not stopped: they
are going on at equal rates, so there is no net change. Equilibrium can only be
reached in a closed system, since if a product escapes the backward reaction
cannot happen.

## The equilibrium constant

For a general reaction

aA + bB reversible cC + dD

the equilibrium constant in terms of concentration is

Kc = ([C]^c x [D]^d) / ([A]^a x [B]^b)

with every concentration measured at equilibrium and in mol/dm3. Products go on
top. Pure solids and pure liquids are left out, because their concentrations do
not change.

For N2 + 3H2 reversible 2NH3, the expression is
Kc = [NH3]^2 / ([N2] x [H2]^3), and its unit is dm6/mol2, because the powers do
not cancel. For H2 + I2 reversible 2HI the powers do cancel, so Kc has no unit.

Worked figure. 1.00 mol of hydrogen and 1.00 mol of iodine vapour are mixed in a
1.00 dm3 vessel. At equilibrium 1.60 mol of hydrogen iodide is present. The
equation is H2 + I2 reversible 2HI, so making 1.60 mol of HI used 0.80 mol of
hydrogen and 0.80 mol of iodine. That leaves 1.00 - 0.80 = 0.20 mol of each, and
in a 1.00 dm3 vessel the concentrations in mol/dm3 are numerically the same as
the amounts. So

Kc = (1.60)^2 / (0.20 x 0.20) = 2.56 / 0.04 = 64

A large Kc means the equilibrium lies well to the right and the reaction goes
nearly to completion; a small Kc means it lies to the left. Kc is changed only by
temperature. A catalyst does not change it.

## Le Chatelier's principle

If a change is imposed on a system at equilibrium, the system adjusts so as to
oppose the change.

- Concentration. Adding more of a reactant shifts the equilibrium to the right,
  using it up. Removing a product as it forms also shifts it to the right.
- Pressure, for gas reactions only. Increasing the pressure shifts the
  equilibrium towards the side with the smaller number of gas molecules. In
  N2 + 3H2 reversible 2NH3 there are 4 molecules on the left and 2 on the right,
  so high pressure favours ammonia. Where the numbers are equal, as in
  H2 + I2 reversible 2HI, pressure has no effect on the position.
- Temperature. Raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium in the endothermic
  direction. The forward reaction in the Haber process is exothermic, so a high
  temperature reduces the yield of ammonia.
- Catalyst. No effect on the position at all; it only brings equilibrium on
  sooner.

## The industrial compromise

In the Haber process, N2 + 3H2 reversible 2NH3, the forward reaction is
exothermic and reduces the number of gas molecules. Yield would be greatest at
low temperature and high pressure, but at a low temperature the reaction is far
too slow. The compromise used in industry is a moderately high temperature of
about 450 degrees Celsius, a high pressure, and an iron catalyst, with the
ammonia removed by liquefaction and the unreacted gases recycled.

In the Contact process, 2SO2 + O2 reversible 2SO3, the same reasoning gives about
450 degrees Celsius, a pressure only modestly above atmospheric because the yield
is already high, and a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst. The sulphur(VI) oxide is
absorbed in concentrated tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid to give oleum, which is then
diluted; it is not absorbed directly in water, because that produces an
unmanageable mist of acid.

## Not on this syllabus

Kp and partial-pressure equilibrium constants, the relation between Kc and Kp,
the variation of K with temperature by the van't Hoff equation, free energy and

formula, and the solubility product are not examined here.
