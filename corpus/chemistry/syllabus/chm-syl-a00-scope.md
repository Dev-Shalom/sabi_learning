## What subject this is

This is the West African Examinations Council (WAEC) Chemistry syllabus for the
WASSCE. It is taken by science candidates alongside Physics and Biology. It is a
different syllabus from any A-level, IB or university chemistry course, and a
question that belongs to one of those courses is out of scope here.

## How the paper is set

Chemistry is examined in three papers. One is an objective paper of
multiple-choice questions drawn from the whole syllabus. One is a theory paper in
two sections: a first section of short compulsory questions, and a second section
of longer questions from which you answer a stated number. One is a practical
paper, or the Alternative to Practical Work paper where a centre cannot run the
practical.

The duration and the mark allocation of each paper are fixed by WAEC for each
sitting and are printed on the question paper itself. Use the current WAEC
regulations for those figures rather than a remembered number.

Marks are given for method. Write the balanced equation, work in moles, then give
the answer with its unit and a sensible number of significant figures.

## The constants and data you are expected to use

Unless a question states otherwise:

- The molar volume of a gas at s.t.p. is 22.4 dm3 per mole. Standard temperature
  and pressure means 273 K (0 degrees Celsius) and 760 mmHg (1 atmosphere).
- The Avogadro constant is 6.02 times 10 to the power 23 per mole.
- The Faraday constant is 96500 coulombs per mole of electrons.
- The molar volume at room temperature and pressure, where a question asks for
  it, is about 24 dm3 per mole. Use 22.4 dm3 only for s.t.p.
- The specific heat capacity of water is 4.2 J per gram per kelvin, which is the
  same as 4200 J per kilogram per kelvin.

Relative atomic masses are supplied by the question. The ones used throughout
this corpus are H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, S = 32,
Cl = 35.5, K = 39, Ca = 40, Fe = 56, Cu = 64, Zn = 65, Ag = 108.

## The topic areas

The syllabus is organised here into these areas:

1. Fundamental principles: the particulate nature of matter, atomic structure,
   chemical bonding, and the periodic table.
2. Quantitative chemistry: the mole concept and stoichiometry, states of matter
   and the gas laws, solutions and solubility, and volumetric analysis.
3. Chemical reactions and energetics: acids, bases and salts, oxidation and
   reduction, electrolysis, energy changes, rates of reaction, and chemical
   equilibrium.
4. Descriptive chemistry: the non-metals and their compounds, and the metals and
   their compounds.
5. Organic chemistry: alkanes, alkenes and alkynes; alkanols, alkanoic acids and
   alkanoates.
6. Chemistry of the environment: air and water pollution, hard water, and the
   chemical industry in West Africa.

## Not on this syllabus

Topics belonging to A-level or university chemistry are not examined here. That
includes crystal-field and ligand-field theory of transition-metal complex ions,
molecular-orbital theory, spectroscopy of any kind (infrared, nuclear magnetic
resonance, mass spectrometry beyond the bare idea of isotopes), detailed reaction
mechanisms with curly arrows and named intermediates, entropy and Gibbs free
energy calculations, the quantitative treatment of buffer solutions by the
Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, rate equations, orders of reaction and
half-life calculations for chemical kinetics, and electrode potential
calculations from standard reduction potentials.
