## What the syllabus requires

Explain why carbon forms so many compounds. Define a homologous series and state
its characteristics. Give the general formula of the alkanes, alkenes and
alkynes, name the first members by IUPAC rules, and draw and name their isomers.
Describe substitution in alkanes and addition in alkenes, give the test for
unsaturation, and describe the cracking of petroleum fractions and the
polymerisation of ethene. Find a molecular formula from a relative molecular
mass.

## Why carbon, and what a homologous series is

Carbon forms an enormous number of compounds because of catenation, the ability
of its atoms to bond to one another in chains and rings of any length, and
because it forms four strong covalent bonds, including double and triple bonds to
itself.

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same general
formula, in which each member differs from the next by CH2, which have similar
chemical properties because they share a functional group, and whose physical
properties change gradually along the series as the molecules get bigger.

## The three series and their names

| Series | General formula | Bond | First member |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alkanes | CnH2n+2 | All single, saturated | Methane, CH4 |
| Alkenes | CnH2n | One double bond, unsaturated | Ethene, C2H4 |
| Alkynes | CnH2n-2 | One triple bond, unsaturated | Ethyne, C2H2 |

The stems are meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-, hept-, oct-, non-, dec- for
one to ten carbon atoms.

To name a branched alkane: find the longest continuous carbon chain and name it;
number it from the end that gives the substituents the lower numbers; name each
branch as a methyl, ethyl and so on, with its number; and put them in
alphabetical order. So CH3CH(CH3)CH2CH3 is 2-methylbutane.

Isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural
formulae. C4H10 has two: butane, a straight chain, and 2-methylpropane, a
branched one. Alkenes can also show position isomerism, since C4H8 can be
but-1-ene or but-2-ene.

Worked figure. An alkene has a relative molecular mass of 56. For CnH2n the mass
is 12n + 2n = 14n, so 14n = 56 gives n = 4 and the molecular formula is C4H8,
butene. The same arithmetic for an alkane uses 14n + 2: a relative molecular mass
of 72 gives 14n = 70, so n = 5 and the formula is C5H12, pentane.

## Reactions

Alkanes are saturated and fairly unreactive. They burn completely in plenty of
air to carbon dioxide and water, and incompletely in a limited supply to
carbon(II) oxide or soot, which is the dangerous case. Their characteristic
reaction is substitution: with chlorine in sunlight, methane gives
chloromethane and hydrogen chloride, and the chlorination continues stepwise to
tetrachloromethane.

Alkenes are unsaturated and much more reactive. Their characteristic reaction is
addition across the double bond: with hydrogen and a nickel catalyst to give the
alkane, which is how vegetable oils are hardened; with a halogen; with hydrogen
halides; and with steam over a catalyst to give an alkanol.

The test for unsaturation is that an alkene decolourises bromine water, or
red-brown bromine in an organic solvent, at room temperature and without
sunlight, by addition. An alkane does not, except slowly in sunlight and then by
substitution, releasing hydrogen bromide. Alkenes also decolourise acidified
potassium tetraoxomanganate(VII) solution.

Ethene undergoes addition polymerisation: many ethene molecules join end to end
to make polyethene. The ethene is the monomer, the polyethene the polymer. Other
addition polymers are polychloroethene from chloroethene and polypropene from
propene.

## Petroleum

Petroleum is a mixture of hydrocarbons separated by fractional distillation into
fractions according to boiling range: refinery gas, petrol, kerosene, diesel oil,
lubricating oil and bitumen, in order of increasing boiling point, increasing
chain length, increasing viscosity and decreasing flammability.

Cracking breaks the less useful long-chain fractions into shorter, more useful
ones, and produces alkenes at the same time. It is done at high temperature,
either thermally or with a catalyst. Reforming rearranges straight chains into
branched and cyclic ones to raise the octane number of petrol.
