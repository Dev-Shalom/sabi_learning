## What the syllabus requires

State Boyle's law, Charles's law and the general gas equation and use them to
calculate a new volume, pressure or temperature for a fixed mass of gas. Convert
between degrees Celsius and kelvin. Define standard temperature and pressure and
correct a gas volume to s.t.p. State Graham's law of diffusion and use it to
compare the rates of diffusion of two gases. State the assumptions of the kinetic
theory for an ideal gas.

## Boyle's law and Charles's law

Boyle's law: at constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is
inversely proportional to its pressure. So P1V1 = P2V2.

Worked figure. A gas occupies 300 cm3 at 100 kPa. Compressed at the same
temperature to 150 kPa, its volume becomes V2 = (100 x 300) / 150 = 200 cm3.

Charles's law: at constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is
directly proportional to its absolute temperature. So V1/T1 = V2/T2, with T in
kelvin.

Temperature in kelvin = temperature in degrees Celsius + 273. So 27 degrees
Celsius is 300 K, and 0 degrees Celsius is 273 K. Absolute zero, 0 K, is
-273 degrees Celsius; it is the temperature at which the volume of an ideal gas
would extrapolate to zero.

Worked figure. A gas occupies 250 cm3 at 273 K. Warmed at constant pressure to
546 K, its volume becomes V2 = 250 x 546 / 273 = 500 cm3. Doubling the absolute
temperature doubles the volume; doubling the Celsius temperature does not.

## The general gas equation and correction to s.t.p.

Combining the two laws for a fixed mass of gas gives

P1V1 / T1 = P2V2 / T2

Standard temperature and pressure, s.t.p., is 273 K and 760 mmHg, which is one
atmosphere or about 101.3 kPa. At s.t.p. one mole of any gas occupies
22.4 dm3.

Worked figure. 250 cm3 of a gas is collected at 27 degrees Celsius (300 K) and
760 mmHg. Corrected to s.t.p. the volume is

V2 = 250 x (273/300) x (760/760) = 250 x 0.91 = 227.5 cm3.

Cooling the gas to 273 K shrinks it; the pressure is unchanged, so that factor is
1. Always substitute kelvin temperatures, and keep the pressures in the same
unit as each other.

## Graham's law of diffusion

Graham's law: at constant temperature and pressure, the rate of diffusion of a
gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its density, and therefore
also to the square root of its relative molecular mass. For two gases,

rate1 / rate2 = square root of (M2 / M1)

Worked figure. Hydrogen, M = 2, and oxygen, M = 32. The ratio of rates is the
square root of 32/2 = the square root of 16 = 4, so hydrogen diffuses four times
as fast as oxygen under the same conditions. Lighter gases diffuse faster.

## The kinetic theory of an ideal gas

An ideal gas is assumed to consist of a very large number of identical particles
in constant random motion; the particles have negligible volume compared with the
volume of the container; there are no attractive or repulsive forces between
them; collisions between them and with the walls are perfectly elastic; and the
average kinetic energy of the particles is proportional to the absolute
temperature.

Real gases depart from ideal behaviour at high pressure and at low temperature,
because under those conditions the particles are close enough for their own
volume and their mutual attractions to matter. That is also why real gases can be
liquefied and an ideal gas could not.

## Not on this syllabus

The ideal gas equation PV = nRT with the gas constant, the van der Waals equation
and its correction terms, the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution treated
mathematically, partial pressures and Dalton's law used quantitatively, critical
temperature and pressure calculations, and the derivation of the kinetic theory
pressure equation are not examined here.
