## What the syllabus requires

Name the three sub-atomic particles with their relative masses and charges.
Define atomic number and mass number and use them to work out the composition of
an atom or ion. Define isotopy and calculate a relative atomic mass from isotopic
abundances. Write the electron configuration of the first twenty elements and
relate it to the element's group and period.

## The sub-atomic particles

| Particle | Relative mass | Relative charge | Where it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proton | 1 | +1 | In the nucleus |
| Neutron | 1 | 0 | In the nucleus |
| Electron | 1/1840, taken as negligible | -1 | In shells around the nucleus |

Almost all the mass of an atom is in its nucleus, because the electron's mass is
about 1/1840 that of a proton. The atom as a whole is neutral, so in a neutral
atom the number of electrons equals the number of protons.

The atomic number Z is the number of protons in the nucleus. It fixes the
identity of the element: every atom with 11 protons is sodium, whatever else is
true of it. The mass number A is the total number of protons and neutrons, so the
number of neutrons is A minus Z.

Sodium is written as sodium-23 with Z = 11, so it has 11 protons, 11 electrons
and 23 - 11 = 12 neutrons. The ion Na+ has lost one electron, so it has 11
protons and 10 electrons and carries a single positive charge. The ion O2- has
gained two electrons, so with Z = 8 it has 8 protons and 10 electrons.

## Isotopes and relative atomic mass

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different
mass numbers, that is, the same number of protons but different numbers of
neutrons. They have identical chemical properties, because chemistry is decided
by the electrons, and slightly different physical properties such as density and
rate of diffusion.

The relative atomic mass of an element is the weighted average of the masses of
its isotopes, taking one-twelfth the mass of a carbon-12 atom as the standard.

Worked figure. Chlorine occurs as 75 per cent chlorine-35 and 25 per cent
chlorine-37. Its relative atomic mass is

(75 x 35 + 25 x 37) / 100 = (2625 + 925) / 100 = 3550 / 100 = 35.5

which is why the relative atomic mass of chlorine is not a whole number even
though every individual atom has a whole-number mass number.

## Electron configuration

Electrons occupy shells of increasing energy. The first shell holds at most 2
electrons, the second at most 8, the third at most 8 for the first twenty
elements, and the fourth begins to fill before the third is completed beyond 8.
So the configurations are written:

- Sodium, Z = 11: 2, 8, 1, or 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1.
- Chlorine, Z = 17: 2, 8, 7, or 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p5.
- Calcium, Z = 20: 2, 8, 8, 2, or 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 4s2.

The number of electrons in the outermost shell gives the group number for the
main-group elements, and the number of occupied shells gives the period. Chlorine
has 7 outer electrons in 3 shells, so it is in Group VII, Period 3. Calcium has
2 outer electrons in 4 shells, so it is in Group II, Period 4.

An atom with a full outer shell, such as neon (2, 8) or argon (2, 8, 8), is
unreactive. Every other atom reacts in a way that gets it to such a
configuration, and that is the whole basis of bonding.

## Not on this syllabus

Quantum numbers and the Schrodinger equation, the shapes of atomic orbitals
beyond naming s and p sub-shells, Hund's rule and spin pairing as formal rules,
successive ionisation energies used to deduce sub-shell structure, mass
spectrometry as an instrumental technique, and nuclear binding energy
calculations are not examined here.
