## What the syllabus requires

Distinguish converging from diverging lenses and define the principal focus, focal
length and optical centre. Draw ray diagrams and use the lens formula and the
magnification formula. Define the power of a lens. Describe the action of the
camera, the simple and compound microscopes, the astronomical telescope and the
projector, and explain the defects of vision and their correction.

## Lenses and the lens formula

A converging (convex) lens is thicker at the middle and brings a parallel beam to
a real principal focus. A diverging (concave) lens is thinner at the middle and
spreads a parallel beam so that it appears to come from a virtual principal focus.

The lens formula is

1/f = 1/u + 1/v,

and the magnification is m = v/u, which also equals the image height divided by
the object height. Using the real-is-positive convention, f is positive for a
converging lens and negative for a diverging lens, and v is negative for a virtual
image.

A converging lens of focal length 20 cm with an object 30 cm away gives
1/v = 1/20 - 1/30 = (3 - 2)/60 = 1/60, so v = 60 cm and m = 60/30 = 2. The image is
real, inverted and twice as large.

The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length in metres, measured in
dioptres: P = 1/f. A converging lens of focal length 25 cm has a power of
1/0.25 = +4 dioptres, and a diverging lens of focal length 50 cm has a power of
-2 dioptres.

## Images from a converging lens

The image depends on where the object sits relative to the focal point F and the
point 2F.

Beyond 2F: the image is real, inverted, diminished, and lies between F and 2F.
This is the camera. At 2F: real, inverted, the same size, at 2F on the other side.
Between F and 2F: real, inverted, magnified, beyond 2F. This is the projector and
the objective of a compound microscope. At F: the image is at infinity. Inside F:
the image is virtual, erect and magnified on the same side as the object. This is
the magnifying glass, also called the simple microscope.

A diverging lens always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image between the lens
and the object, whatever the object position.

## Optical instruments and the eye

In a camera a converging lens throws a real, inverted, diminished image onto the
film or sensor; focusing is done by moving the lens, and the aperture controls the
light admitted.

A compound microscope uses two converging lenses of short focal length. The
objective forms a real, magnified image just inside the focal point of the
eyepiece, and the eyepiece acts as a magnifying glass on that image, so the final
image is virtual, inverted relative to the object, and very large.

An astronomical telescope uses an objective of long focal length and an eyepiece
of short focal length. In normal adjustment the separation of the lenses is the
sum of their focal lengths and the magnification is the focal length of the
objective divided by the focal length of the eyepiece. An objective of 100 cm with
an eyepiece of 5 cm gives a magnification of 20 and a tube length of 105 cm.

In the human eye the cornea and the lens form a real, inverted, diminished image
on the retina, and focusing is done by the ciliary muscles changing the shape of
the lens, a process called accommodation.

Short sight (myopia) is the inability to see distant objects clearly, because the
image forms in front of the retina; it is corrected with a diverging lens. Long
sight (hypermetropia) is the inability to see near objects clearly, because the
image would form behind the retina; it is corrected with a converging lens.
Astigmatism is corrected with a cylindrical lens, and presbyopia, the loss of
accommodation with age, with bifocals.

## Not on this syllabus

The lens-maker's equation in terms of surface curvature and refractive index,
spherical and chromatic aberration treated quantitatively, ray-transfer matrix
methods, and the design of multi-element lens systems are not examined here.
