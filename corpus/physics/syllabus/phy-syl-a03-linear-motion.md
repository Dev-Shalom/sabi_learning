## What the syllabus requires

Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity. Define
acceleration. Use the equations of uniformly accelerated motion to solve problems,
including free fall under gravity. Read and draw displacement-time and
velocity-time graphs, taking velocity from the gradient of one and distance from
the area under the other.

## The quantities

Distance is the total path length travelled, a scalar. Displacement is the
straight-line change of position with direction, a vector. A runner who completes
one lap of a 400 m track has travelled a distance of 400 m but has a displacement
of zero.

Speed is distance per unit time, a scalar. Velocity is displacement per unit time,
a vector. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, in m/s squared. A
negative acceleration in the direction of motion is a retardation.

## The equations of motion

For motion in a straight line with uniform acceleration, with u the initial
velocity, v the final velocity, a the acceleration, t the time and s the
displacement:

1. v = u + at
2. s = ut + one half a t squared
3. v squared = u squared + 2as
4. s = one half (u + v) t

Choose the equation that contains the three quantities you know and the one you
want. A body starting at 5 m/s and accelerating at 2 m/s squared for 4 s reaches
v = 5 + 2(4) = 13 m/s and covers s = 5(4) + one half (2)(16) = 20 + 16 = 36 m.

Keep signs consistent. Choose one direction as positive and give every quantity
its sign in that frame. A retardation of 6 m/s squared in motion taken as positive
is written a = -6 m/s squared.

## Motion under gravity

A body falling freely has a = g downwards, and this syllabus takes
g = 10 m/s squared unless the question says otherwise. The mass of the body does
not appear in any of the four equations, so in the absence of air resistance a
heavy body and a light body fall together.

For a body projected vertically upward with speed u, take upward as positive so
that a = -g. It rises for a time u/g, reaches a maximum height u squared divided by
2g, and returns to the point of projection after a total time 2u/g with a speed
equal in magnitude to u. A stone thrown up at 30 m/s rises for 3 s to a height of
900/20 = 45 m and is back after 6 s.

## Motion graphs

On a displacement-time graph the gradient is the velocity. A straight line means
uniform velocity; a curve of increasing gradient means acceleration.

On a velocity-time graph the gradient is the acceleration and the area under the
graph is the distance travelled. A body that accelerates uniformly from rest to
20 m/s in 5 s, holds 20 m/s for 10 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 5 s
covers one half (5)(20) + 20(10) + one half (5)(20) = 50 + 200 + 50 = 300 m, and
its retardation in the last stage is 20/5 = 4 m/s squared.

## Not on this syllabus

Deriving the equations of motion by integrating a variable acceleration, motion
against a resistive force proportional to speed, terminal-velocity differential
equations, and relativistic addition of velocities are not examined here.
