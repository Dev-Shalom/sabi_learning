## What the syllabus requires

Define density and relative density and measure them. Define pressure and
calculate the pressure in a liquid column. State Pascal's principle and apply it
to the hydraulic press. Describe the measurement of atmospheric pressure. State
Archimedes' principle and the law of flotation and use them.

## Density and relative density

Density is mass per unit volume, rho = m/V, in kg per cubic metre. Water has a
density of 1000 kg per cubic metre, which is the same as 1 g per cubic centimetre.
To convert, 1 g/cm cubed = 1000 kg/m cubed.

A block of mass 270 g and volume 100 cubic centimetres has a density of
270/100 = 2.7 g per cubic centimetre, that is 2700 kg per cubic metre.

Relative density is the density of a substance divided by the density of water. It
has no unit. For that block the relative density is 2.7. Equivalently, relative
density is the mass of any volume of the substance divided by the mass of an equal
volume of water.

## Pressure

Pressure is force per unit area, P = F/A, in pascals, where 1 Pa = 1 N per square
metre. The same force gives a larger pressure on a smaller area, which is why a
sharp knife cuts and a wide tyre sinks less into soft ground.

The pressure at a depth h in a liquid of density rho is

P = rho g h.

It depends only on the depth, the density and g, and not at all on the shape or
the cross-sectional area of the vessel. At a depth of 5 m in water,
P = 1000(10)(5) = 50000 Pa. Pressure in a fluid acts equally in all directions at
a given depth, which is why a liquid finds the same level in every arm of a
connected vessel.

## Pascal's principle and the hydraulic press

Pascal's principle states that pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is
transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid and to the walls of the
container.

In a hydraulic press an effort F1 on a small piston of area A1 sets up a pressure
F1 divided by A1, and that same pressure acts on a large piston of area A2, giving
a force F2 = F1 times A2 divided by A1.

A force of 20 N on a piston of area 0.001 square metres gives a pressure of
20000 Pa, and on a piston of area 0.05 square metres that produces
20000(0.05) = 1000 N. The large piston moves correspondingly less, so no energy is
created. The hydraulic brake and the hydraulic jack work this way.

## Atmospheric pressure, upthrust and flotation

Atmospheric pressure is measured with a mercury barometer and is about 760 mm of
mercury at sea level, which is roughly 1.0 times 10 to the power 5 Pa. Since
P = rho g h, a barometer using water instead of mercury would need a tube more
than 10 m tall, because water is about 13.6 times less dense than mercury.

Archimedes' principle states that a body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid
experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. The
apparent loss in weight of the body equals that upthrust: a body weighing 6 N in
air and 4 N in water experiences an upthrust of 2 N.

The law of flotation states that a floating body displaces its own weight of the
fluid in which it floats. A body floats if its density is less than that of the
fluid, sinks if it is greater, and stays wherever it is placed if the two are
equal. A ship made of steel floats because its overall density, counting the air
inside the hull, is less than that of water.
