## What the syllabus requires

State Boyle's law, Charles's law and the pressure law and use each one. Combine
them into the general gas equation and use it. Convert Celsius temperatures to
kelvin before every gas calculation. Explain the pressure of a gas using the
kinetic theory, and state the assumptions of that theory.

## The three gas laws

Boyle's law: at constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is
inversely proportional to its pressure. So PV is constant, and

P1 V1 = P2 V2.

A gas of volume 400 cubic centimetres at 1.0 times 10 to the power 5 Pa
compressed to 250 cubic centimetres at the same temperature reaches
P2 = 1.0 times 10 to the power 5 times 400 divided by 250, which is
1.6 times 10 to the power 5 Pa.

Charles's law: at constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly
proportional to its absolute temperature. So V divided by T is constant, and

V1 divided by T1 = V2 divided by T2.

A gas of volume 300 cubic centimetres at 27 degrees Celsius (300 K) heated to
127 degrees Celsius (400 K) at constant pressure expands to
300 times 400 divided by 300 = 400 cubic centimetres.

The pressure law: at constant volume, the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is
directly proportional to its absolute temperature, so P1 divided by T1 = P2
divided by T2.

## The general gas equation

Combining the three laws for a fixed mass of gas gives

P1 V1 divided by T1 = P2 V2 divided by T2,

and for an ideal gas PV = nRT, where n is the number of moles and R is the molar
gas constant.

Every temperature in these equations must be in kelvin. Using degrees Celsius is
the commonest mistake in this topic and it makes the answer nonsense, because the
laws are statements of proportionality to absolute temperature.

Volumes and pressures need only be in consistent units on both sides: if V1 is in
cubic centimetres then V2 comes out in cubic centimetres, and the pressure units
cancel.

## The kinetic theory of gases

The kinetic theory treats a gas as a very large number of identical molecules in
rapid random motion. Its assumptions are that the molecules are so small that
their own volume is negligible compared with the volume of the container, that
there are no forces between them except during collisions, that all collisions
with each other and with the walls are perfectly elastic, that the time of a
collision is negligible compared with the time between collisions, and that they
obey Newton's laws.

Pressure arises because the molecules bombard the walls of the container; each
impact changes the momentum of a molecule, so each impact exerts a force on the
wall, and the pressure is the total force per unit area averaged over time.

The theory explains the gas laws directly. Reducing the volume packs the same
number of molecules into a smaller space, so there are more impacts per second per
unit area and the pressure rises, which is Boyle's law. Raising the absolute
temperature raises the average kinetic energy and therefore the average speed of
the molecules, so they strike the walls harder and more often, which is the
pressure law; if the container can expand, the volume grows instead, which is
Charles's law.

The absolute temperature of a gas is a measure of the average kinetic energy of
its molecules, and at absolute zero that average kinetic energy would be zero.

## Not on this syllabus

The van der Waals equation and real-gas corrections, the Maxwell-Boltzmann
distribution of molecular speeds, the derivation of PV = one third N m c squared
