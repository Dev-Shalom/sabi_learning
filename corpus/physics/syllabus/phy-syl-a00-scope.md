## What subject this is

This is the West African Examinations Council (WAEC) Physics syllabus for the
WASSCE. It is taken by science candidates alongside Chemistry and Biology. It is
a different syllabus from any A-level, IB or university physics course, and a
question that belongs to one of those courses is out of scope here.

## How the paper is set

Physics is examined in three papers. One is an objective paper of multiple-choice
questions drawn from the whole syllabus. One is a theory paper in two sections: a
first section of short compulsory questions, and a second section of longer
questions from which you answer a stated number. One is a practical paper, or the
Alternative to Practical Work paper where a centre cannot run the practical.

The duration and the mark allocation of each paper are fixed by WAEC for each
sitting and are printed on the question paper itself. Use the current WAEC
regulations for those figures rather than a remembered number.

In the theory paper marks are given for method. Write the formula, substitute the
values with their units, then give the answer with its unit.

## The constants you are expected to use

Unless a question states otherwise, take the acceleration of free fall as
g = 10 m/s squared. Every worked example in this corpus uses g = 10 m/s squared
and says so in the solution. Where a question supplies a different value, such as
9.8 m/s squared, use the value the question gives.

Other values are normally supplied by the question rather than recalled: the
specific heat capacity of water (4200 J/kg/K), the specific latent heat of fusion
of ice (336000 J/kg), the speed of sound in air (about 330 to 340 m/s), the speed
of light in vacuum (3 times 10 to the power 8 m/s), and the Planck constant
(6.6 times 10 to the power -34 J s).

## The topic areas

The syllabus is organised here into these areas:

1. Measurement and mechanics: units and measurement, scalars and vectors, linear
   motion, projectiles, circular motion, Newton's laws and momentum.
2. Energy, work and machines: work, energy, power, energy conversion, and simple
   machines.
3. Matter, pressure and heat: density and pressure, fluids and flotation,
   temperature and thermal expansion, heat capacity and latent heat, gas laws.
4. Waves, sound and light: wave motion, sound, reflection and refraction, lenses
   and optical instruments.
5. Fields, electricity and magnetism: electrostatics, current electricity,
   magnetism and electromagnetic induction.
6. Modern physics and electronics: basic electronics, atomic structure, the
   photoelectric effect, and radioactivity.

## Not on this syllabus

Topics belonging to A-level or university physics are not examined here. That
includes calculus-based derivations of the equations of motion, Maxwell's
equations, special and general relativity beyond the bare statement that
E = mc squared, quantum mechanics beyond the photoelectric equation and the de
Broglie relation, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, statistical mechanics and
entropy, rotational dynamics using moment of inertia, and the standard model of
particle physics.
