## What the syllabus requires

Define mechanical advantage, velocity ratio and efficiency, and calculate them for
the common machines: levers, pulleys, the inclined plane, the wheel and axle, the
screw jack and gear wheels. Explain why the efficiency of a real machine is always
less than 100 per cent.

## The three quantities

A machine is a device that lets a small effort overcome a large load, or that
changes the direction in which a force is applied.

Mechanical advantage (MA) is the load divided by the effort. It has no unit. A
machine that lifts a 600 N load with an effort of 150 N has MA = 4.

Velocity ratio (VR) is the distance moved by the effort divided by the distance
moved by the load in the same time. It has no unit and it depends only on the
geometry of the machine, never on friction or on the weight of its parts.

Efficiency is the useful work got out divided by the work put in, expressed as a
percentage. Since work out is load times load distance and work in is effort times
effort distance,

efficiency = (MA divided by VR) times 100 per cent.

If MA = 4 and VR = 5, the efficiency is (4/5)(100) = 80 per cent.

## Levers and the principle of moments

A lever turns about a pivot, and for it the principle of moments gives
load times load arm = effort times effort arm, so the velocity ratio is the effort
arm divided by the load arm.

In a first-order lever the pivot is between the load and the effort: a pair of
scissors, a crowbar, a see-saw. In a second-order lever the load is between the
pivot and the effort: a wheelbarrow, a nutcracker, a bottle opener. In a
third-order lever the effort is between the pivot and the load: a pair of tongs, a
fishing rod, the human forearm.

## Pulleys, planes, wheels and screws

For a block and tackle the velocity ratio equals the number of rope sections
supporting the movable block. A system with four supporting sections has VR = 4;
if it lifts a 400 N load with a 125 N effort, MA = 3.2 and the efficiency is
(3.2/4)(100) = 80 per cent.

For an inclined plane of length l and vertical height h, the velocity ratio is
l divided by h. A plank 10 m long raising a load 2 m has VR = 5.

For a wheel and axle the velocity ratio is the radius of the wheel divided by the
radius of the axle. A wheel of radius 40 cm on an axle of radius 8 cm has VR = 5.

For a screw jack of pitch p driven by a handle of length r, one turn of the handle
moves the effort a distance 2 pi r while the load rises by one pitch, so
VR = 2 pi r divided by p. With r = 35 cm and p = 0.2 cm, taking pi as 22/7,
VR = 2(22/7)(35)/0.2 = 220/0.2 = 1100.

For two meshed gear wheels the velocity ratio is the number of teeth on the driven
wheel divided by the number of teeth on the driving wheel.

## Why efficiency is never 100 per cent

Part of the work put into a real machine is spent overcoming friction between the
moving parts and lifting the weight of the moving parts themselves, and that work
does not appear as useful output. Efficiency is therefore always below 100 per
cent, and a machine whose efficiency is quoted as 100 per cent or more has been
measured wrongly. Oiling the bearings and using lighter moving parts raise the
efficiency but can never remove the loss.

A machine does not multiply energy or work. When MA is large, VR is large too and
the effort simply moves much further than the load.

## Not on this syllabus

The efficiency of heat engines, the Carnot cycle, gear-train dynamics using torque
and angular acceleration, and the analysis of linkages beyond simple lever systems
are not examined in this paper.
