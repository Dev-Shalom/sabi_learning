## What the syllabus requires

Define work, energy and power and give their units. Calculate work done by a
force, including a force at an angle to the displacement. Use the formulae for
kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy. State and apply the principle
of conservation of energy. Identify energy conversions in everyday devices and
distinguish renewable from non-renewable energy sources.

## Work

Work is done when a force moves its point of application along the direction of
the force. For a constant force F moving a body a distance d in the direction of
the force, W = Fd, measured in joules. One joule is one newton metre.

If the force acts at an angle theta to the displacement, only the component along
the displacement does work, so W = F d cos theta. A force of 50 N pulling a box
10 m along the ground with the rope at 60 degrees to the horizontal does
50(10)(cos 60) = 250 J of work.

No work is done when the force is perpendicular to the motion (cos 90 = 0), which
is why a body carried horizontally, or a satellite in a circular orbit, has no
work done on it by the vertical force or by gravity respectively.

Work done against gravity in lifting a body of mass m through a height h is mgh.
Lifting 20 kg through 3 m with g = 10 m/s squared takes 20(10)(3) = 600 J.

## Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work, also measured in joules.

Kinetic energy is the energy of a moving body: KE = one half m v squared. A 4 kg
body moving at 5 m/s has KE = one half (4)(25) = 50 J. Doubling the speed
quadruples the kinetic energy, which is why stopping distances grow so quickly
with speed.

Gravitational potential energy is the energy of position: PE = mgh, measured from
a chosen reference level. A 2 kg body 8 m above the ground has
PE = 2(10)(8) = 160 J.

The principle of conservation of energy says that energy can be converted from one
form to another but is never created or destroyed; the total stays constant. For a
body falling freely the loss in potential energy equals the gain in kinetic
energy, so mgh = one half m v squared and v squared = 2gh, with the mass
cancelling. Falling from 8 m gives v squared = 160 and v = 12.6 m/s.

## Power

Power is the rate of doing work, P = W/t, measured in watts. One watt is one joule
per second. A man of mass 60 kg who climbs 5 m in 10 s does 60(10)(5) = 3000 J of
work and develops 3000/10 = 300 W.

For a body moving at constant velocity v against a constant resisting force F, the
power is P = Fv. A vehicle moving at 20 m/s against a total resistance of 900 N
needs an engine power of 900(20) = 18000 W, that is 18 kW.

## Energy conversions and sources

Devices are energy converters. A battery turns chemical energy into electrical
energy; a lamp turns electrical energy into light and heat; a loudspeaker turns
electrical energy into sound; a generator turns mechanical energy into electrical
energy; a motor does the reverse; a hydroelectric station turns the potential
energy of stored water into electrical energy.

Renewable sources can be replaced as fast as they are used: solar, wind, hydro,
tidal, geothermal and biomass. Non-renewable sources cannot: coal, crude oil,
natural gas and nuclear fuel.

## Not on this syllabus

The work-energy theorem derived by integrating a variable force, potential-energy
functions with force equal to the negative gradient of potential, elastic
potential energy beyond the simple statement of Hooke's law, and thermodynamic
free energy are beyond this paper.
