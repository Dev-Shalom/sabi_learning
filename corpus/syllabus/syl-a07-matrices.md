## What the syllabus requires

Identify the order of a matrix. Add, subtract and multiply matrices up to order
3 by 3. Find the determinant and the inverse of a 2 by 2 matrix. Use matrices to
solve two simultaneous linear equations.

## Order and equality

The order of a matrix is rows by columns. Two matrices are equal only if they have
the same order and every corresponding entry is equal.

## Addition and multiplication

Add and subtract entry by entry; this needs matrices of the same order. To
multiply, the number of columns of the first must equal the number of rows of the
second: row times column gives each entry. Matrix multiplication is not
commutative, so AB and BA are usually different.

## Determinant of a 2 by 2 matrix

For the matrix with entries a and b in the first row and c and d in the second,
the determinant is ad - bc.

## Inverse of a 2 by 2 matrix

The inverse is 1 divided by the determinant, multiplied by the matrix with entries
d and negative b in the first row, and negative c and a in the second. The inverse
exists only when the determinant is not zero; such a matrix is called singular
when the determinant is zero.

## Solving simultaneous equations

Write the pair of equations as a matrix equation AX = B. Then X = A inverse times
B, provided the determinant of A is not zero.

## Not on this syllabus

Determinants and inverses of matrices of order 3 by 3, row reduction, eigenvalues
and matrix transformations beyond the simple cases in the transformation topic are
Further Mathematics.
