## What the syllabus requires

The four operations on fractions and decimals. Convert between fractions,
decimals and percentages. Approximate to a given number of decimal places or
significant figures. Express a number in standard form. Calculate percentage
error.

## Significant figures and decimal places

Decimal places are counted after the point. Significant figures are counted from
the first non-zero digit. In 0.004073, the first significant figure is 4, so to
two significant figures it is 0.0041, and to three decimal places it is 0.004.

Zeros between non-zero digits are always significant. Leading zeros never are.

## Standard form

A number in standard form is written as A times 10 to the power n, where A is at
least 1 and less than 10, and n is an integer. So 0.000352 is 3.52 times 10 to the
power negative 4, and 47 300 is 4.73 times 10 to the power 4.

## Percentage error

Percentage error is the error divided by the true or exact value, times 100. The
error is the difference between the approximate value and the exact value, taken
as a positive number.

If a length is measured as 4.5 m when it is truly 4.6 m, the error is 0.1 m and the
percentage error is (0.1 / 4.6) times 100, about 2.17 per cent.

## Rounding rules in the exam

Unless the question says otherwise, give a final answer correct to three
significant figures, and do not round intermediate working. Rounding early is one
of the commonest ways to lose accuracy marks.
