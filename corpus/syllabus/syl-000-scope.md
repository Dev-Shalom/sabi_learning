## What subject this is

This is the West African Examinations Council (WAEC) General Mathematics syllabus,
also called Core Mathematics or simply Mathematics. Every candidate for the WASSCE
takes it. It is a different syllabus from Further Mathematics, which is an extra
subject taken mostly by science students.

## How the paper is set

There are two papers, written in one sitting, for a total of 100 marks.

Paper 1 is objective: 50 multiple-choice questions covering the whole syllabus, to
be answered in 1 hour 30 minutes, worth 40 marks.

Paper 2 is theory, 2 hours 30 minutes, worth 60 marks, and comes in two sections.
Section A has five compulsory questions on the core of the syllabus. Section B has
eight questions, of which you answer five. In Section B you must show your working:
marks are given for method, not only for the final answer.

## The topic areas

The syllabus is organised into these areas:

1. Number and numeration: number bases, fractions and approximation, indices,
   logarithms, sets, surds, sequences and series, matrices, ratio and proportion,
   percentages, financial arithmetic, variation.
2. Algebraic processes: algebraic expressions and fractions, linear equations and
   inequalities, change of subject, quadratic equations, functions and relations,
   graphs.
3. Mensuration: lengths, perimeters, areas, volumes, and the geometry of the earth
   as a sphere.
4. Plane geometry: angles, triangles and polygons, circle theorems, construction,
   loci.
5. Trigonometry: the sine, cosine and tangent ratios, the sine and cosine rules,
   angles of elevation and depression, bearings.
6. Introductory calculus: differentiation and integration of simple algebraic
   functions.
7. Statistics and probability.
8. Vectors and transformation in the Cartesian plane.

## Not on this syllabus

Further Mathematics topics are not examined here. That includes complex numbers,
matrices larger than 3 by 3, row reduction, differentiation of trigonometric,
exponential or logarithmic functions, integration by parts or by substitution,
differential equations, conic sections, binomial expansion for a non-integer or
negative index, permutations and combinations beyond simple counting, and
hypothesis testing.
