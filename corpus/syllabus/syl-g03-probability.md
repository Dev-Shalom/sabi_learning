## What the syllabus requires

Experimental and theoretical probability. Addition of probabilities for mutually
exclusive and independent events. Multiplication of probabilities for independent
events. Problems involving coins, dice, cards and balls in a bag, with and without
replacement.

## Basic definition

The probability of an event is the number of favourable outcomes divided by the
number of possible outcomes, when all outcomes are equally likely. Every
probability lies between 0 and 1.

The probability that an event does not happen is 1 minus the probability that it
does.

## Addition rule

For mutually exclusive events, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Mutually exclusive means
the two cannot both happen.

If they are not mutually exclusive, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B).

## Multiplication rule

For independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) times P(B). Independent means the first
event does not affect the second.

## With and without replacement

With replacement, the denominator stays the same for the second draw and the
events are independent. Without replacement, the denominator falls by one and the
numerator may also change. Read the question carefully: this single word changes
the whole answer.

## Not on this syllabus

Conditional probability with the formal Bayes formula, probability distributions,
and expectation are Further Mathematics.
