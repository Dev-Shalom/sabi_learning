## What the syllabus requires

Application of the sine rule and the cosine rule to triangles that are not
right-angled, and the calculation of the area of a triangle using two sides and
the included angle.

## The sine rule

a over sin A = b over sin B = c over sin C.

Use it when you have two angles and one side, or two sides and an angle opposite
one of them.

## The cosine rule

a squared = b squared + c squared - 2bc cos A.

Use it when you have two sides and the included angle, or all three sides. In the
three-sides case, rearrange to
cos A = (b squared + c squared - a squared) divided by 2bc.

## Area of a triangle

Area = half ab sin C, using two sides and the angle between them.

## Choosing between them

If the triangle has a right angle, use the simple ratios instead; both rules still
work but they are slower. If the cosine of an angle comes out negative, the angle
is obtuse, and that is a legitimate answer, not a mistake.
