## What the syllabus requires

Distance along a great circle and along a parallel of latitude, on the earth
treated as a sphere of radius 6400 km. Difference in longitude and latitude, and
simple problems on the shortest distance between two points.

## The two kinds of circle

A great circle has the same centre and radius as the earth. All meridians of
longitude, and the equator, are great circles. A parallel of latitude other than
the equator is a small circle, and its radius is R cos(theta), where theta is the
latitude.

## Distances

Along a meridian or along the equator, distance = (angle difference over 360)
times 2 pi R.

Along a parallel of latitude theta, distance = (longitude difference over 360)
times 2 pi R cos theta.

## Conventions

Latitudes north and south of the equator, longitudes east and west of the
Greenwich meridian. When two places are on opposite sides, add the angles; when on
the same side, subtract. Take R as 6400 km and pi as 22 over 7 unless told
otherwise.
