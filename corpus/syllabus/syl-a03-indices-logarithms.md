## What the syllabus requires

Apply the laws of indices. Solve equations involving indices. Relate indices to
logarithms in base ten. Apply the laws of logarithms to calculation, including the
use of logarithm tables.

## Laws of indices

a^m times a^n = a^(m+n).
a^m divided by a^n = a^(m-n).
(a^m)^n = a^(mn).
a^0 = 1 for any a that is not zero.
a^(-n) = 1 divided by a^n.
a^(1/n) is the nth root of a, and a^(m/n) is the nth root of a, raised to the
power m.

## Solving index equations

Write both sides with the same base and equate the powers. If 3^(2x-1) = 81, then
81 is 3^4, so 2x - 1 = 4 and x = 2.5.

## Logarithms in base ten

If 10^y = x then log x = y, where log means logarithm to base ten. Logarithm and
index are two ways of saying the same thing, which is why log 1000 = 3.

## Laws of logarithms

log(MN) = log M + log N.
log(M/N) = log M - log N.
log(M^n) = n log M.
log 1 = 0 and log 10 = 1.

## The characteristic and the mantissa

When using four-figure tables, a logarithm is written as a whole-number part (the
characteristic) plus a decimal part (the mantissa). The characteristic of a number
between 1 and 10 is 0, between 10 and 100 it is 1, and so on. For a number less
than 1 the characteristic is negative and is written with a bar above it, as in
bar 2 point 6021 for log 0.04.

## Not on this syllabus

Logarithms to bases other than ten, the change of base formula, and natural
logarithms to base e belong to Further Mathematics, not to this paper.
