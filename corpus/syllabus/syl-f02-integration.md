## What the syllabus requires

Integration of simple algebraic functions as the reverse of differentiation.
Evaluation of simple definite integrals. Area under a curve.

## The rule

The integral of ax^n with respect to x is a times x^(n + 1) divided by (n + 1),
plus a constant of integration C, valid for every n except negative 1.

## Definite integrals

Integrate, then substitute the upper limit and subtract the value at the lower
limit. The constant of integration cancels, so it is not written in a definite
integral.

## Area under a curve

The area between the curve y = f(x), the x axis, and the lines x = a and x = b is
the definite integral of f(x) from a to b, provided the curve does not cross the
axis between the limits. Where it does cross, work out the parts separately and
add the magnitudes, otherwise the positive and negative areas cancel.

## Not on this syllabus

Integration by parts and integration by substitution are not on this syllabus.
Integrating trigonometric functions such as sin x and cos x, and exponential
functions such as e to the power x, is not on this syllabus either. Neither are
volumes of revolution, nor differential equations.

All of these belong to Further Mathematics, which is a separate subject with its
own paper.
