## What the syllabus requires

Idea of a set, set notation, subsets, the universal set, union, intersection and
complement. Venn diagrams for two and three sets, and the use of Venn diagrams to
solve problems involving classification and counting.

## Notation

The number of elements of a set A is written n(A). The empty set has no elements.
A is a subset of B when every element of A is also in B. The complement of A,
written A prime, is everything in the universal set that is not in A.

## Union and intersection

A union B contains everything that is in A, or in B, or in both. A intersection B
contains only what is in both.

## Counting formula for two sets

n(A union B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A intersection B).

The subtraction is there because elements in both sets have been counted twice.

## Three-set problems

For three sets, fill the Venn diagram from the centre outwards. Put the number in
all three sets first, then the pairwise regions after subtracting the centre, then
the single regions after subtracting everything already placed. Only then use the
total. Working outside-in is the usual source of mistakes.

The formula is n(A union B union C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - n(A and B)
- n(A and C) - n(B and C) + n(A and B and C).

## Not on this syllabus

Infinite set theory, cardinality of infinite sets, and formal proofs about sets
are not examined here.
