## What the syllabus requires

Solve quadratic equations by factorisation, by completing the square and by
formula. Form a quadratic equation from given roots. Use the sum and product of
roots. Draw and interpret the graph of a quadratic function, including the maximum
or minimum point, the line of symmetry, and the roots read from the graph.

## The quadratic formula

For ax^2 + bx + c = 0 with a not zero,
x = [ -b plus or minus the square root of (b^2 - 4ac) ] divided by 2a.

## The discriminant

The discriminant is b^2 - 4ac. If it is positive there are two distinct real
roots. If it is zero there is one repeated root. If it is negative there are no
real roots, and in this paper the answer is that the equation has no real solution.

## Completing the square

Make the coefficient of x^2 equal to 1, move the constant to the right, add the
square of half the coefficient of x to both sides, then take the square root of
both sides. Completing the square also gives the turning point directly: written
as a(x + p)^2 + q, the turning point is at x = -p, y = q.

## Sum and product of roots

If the roots are alpha and beta, then alpha + beta = -b/a and alpha times beta =
c/a. An equation with these roots can be written as
x^2 - (sum of roots)x + (product of roots) = 0.

## The graph

The graph of a quadratic is a parabola. It opens upwards when a is positive, and
then the turning point is a minimum; it opens downwards when a is negative, and
the turning point is a maximum. The roots are where the curve cuts the x axis. The
line of symmetry is x = -b divided by 2a.
