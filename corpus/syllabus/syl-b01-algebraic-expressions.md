## What the syllabus requires

Expansion and factorisation of algebraic expressions. Operations on algebraic
fractions with monomial and binomial denominators. Simplification and the
substitution of values.

## Expansion

Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second.
(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd.

Three expansions are worth memorising because they appear constantly:
(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2;
(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2;
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2, the difference of two squares.

## Factorisation

Look in this order: a common factor first, then difference of two squares, then a
perfect square, then grouping in pairs, then the product-sum method for a
quadratic trinomial.

For ax^2 + bx + c, find two numbers whose product is ac and whose sum is b, split
the middle term with them, then factorise in pairs.

## Algebraic fractions

Add and subtract by finding the lowest common multiple of the denominators, in the
same way as with numerical fractions. Multiply by cancelling common factors first.
Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.

An algebraic fraction is undefined where its denominator is zero, and this is worth
stating when a question asks for values that must be excluded.

## Not on this syllabus

Partial fractions and polynomial long division beyond simple cases belong to
Further Mathematics.
