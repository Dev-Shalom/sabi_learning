## What the syllabus requires

Differentiation of algebraic functions. The gradient of a curve at a point.
Equations of tangents and normals is not required, but rate of change, and
maximum and minimum points, are.

## The rule

If y = ax^n then dy/dx = anx^(n - 1). The derivative of a constant is zero.
Differentiate a sum term by term.

## What the derivative means

dy/dx is the gradient of the curve at a point, and it is also the rate of change
of y with respect to x. For distance against time, the derivative is velocity, and
the derivative of velocity is acceleration.

## Maximum and minimum

At a turning point dy/dx = 0. Solve this to find the x value, then substitute back
into y to find the point. To decide which kind of point it is, use the second
derivative: if d2y/dx2 is negative the point is a maximum, if positive it is a
minimum.

## Not on this syllabus

Differentiation of trigonometric functions is not on this syllabus. You will not
be asked to differentiate sin x, cos x or tan x on this paper. Differentiating
exponential functions such as e to the power x, and logarithmic functions such
as ln x or log x, is also not on this syllabus.

The product rule, the quotient rule and the chain rule for anything beyond a
simple power are not required either.

All of these belong to Further Mathematics, which is a separate subject with its
own paper.
