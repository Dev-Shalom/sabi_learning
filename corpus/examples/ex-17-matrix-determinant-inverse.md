## Question

Given the matrix A with first row 2 and 3, and second row 1 and 4, find (a) the
determinant of A, (b) the inverse of A.

## Solution

Part (a)

1. For a matrix with first row a, b and second row c, d, the determinant is
   ad - bc.
2. Determinant of A = (2)(4) - (3)(1) = 8 - 3 = 5.

**Answer (a): 5.**

Part (b)

3. The inverse is 1 over the determinant, multiplied by the matrix whose first
   row is d and negative b, and whose second row is negative c and a.
4. Swap the leading diagonal entries, 2 and 4, and change the signs of 3 and 1.
5. So A inverse = one fifth times the matrix with first row 4 and -3, second row
   -1 and 2.

**Answer (b): A inverse has entries 4/5 and -3/5 in the first row, -1/5 and 2/5
in the second.**

## Check

Multiplying A by A inverse gives the identity matrix, with 1s on the leading
diagonal and 0s elsewhere.
