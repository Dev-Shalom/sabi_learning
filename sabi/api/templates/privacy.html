What Sabi stores about you

Most people using Sabi are under 18, so it collects as little as it can get away with. Here is the whole list, in plain words.

An account is optional

You can ask questions with no account at all, the way Sabi has always worked. Your browser holds a random id that Sabi made up — something like 7f3c… with no meaning. That id is how it counts your {{ free_cap }} questions a day. Clear your browser data and the id is gone and you are a new person to Sabi.

Making an account is optional. It raises your limit to {{ account_cap }} questions a day and keeps the questions you have already asked, so you can see which topics you keep getting stuck on. Nothing on this site refuses to work because you did not make one.

If you do make an account

Two things are stored: the email address you type in, and your password after it has been put through scrypt, which turns it into a long string that cannot be turned back into your password. Nobody running Sabi can read it, and nobody reading the database can either.

Sabi cannot send email and never does. Your address is used to log you in and for nothing else — no newsletters, no reset links, no messages of any kind. That is also why you were given recovery codes when you signed up: they are the only way back in if you forget your password.

Your questions

The question you type and the answer you get are stored, attached to your account if you have one and to that random id if you do not. They are kept so that when you mark an answer wrong, the mistake can actually be found and fixed. Your questions are never shown to your school.

Your network address

Sabi stores a scrambled version of your IP address, not the address itself. The scrambling is one way and uses a secret salt, so it cannot be turned back into the original. It exists only to stop one person clearing their browser over and over to get unlimited free questions.

Feedback

When you tap Yes or No on an answer, that is stored against the answer and against whoever asked it, and nothing else.

The waitlist

Only if you type your email into the waitlist yourself. It is used for one thing: telling you if the daily limit changes. You can use Sabi without ever touching it.

What Sabi never does

No adverts. Nothing sold to anyone, ever. No trackers from other companies. No location. No profile built to follow you around other websites.

Sabi can be wrong. It is a study aid, not a teacher and not the exam board. For anything that matters, your teacher and the official WAEC syllabus are the authority.
